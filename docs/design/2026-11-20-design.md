# sp500-factor-ml-portfolio: design

Status: approved by the owner, 2026-11-20. Project #5 of the portfolio plan (`../PORTFOLIO_PLAN.md`).

## 1. Intent and why

The owner is a financial-engineering student applying for an M2 internship in France (asset
management, private banking, data roles with a finance focus). A recruiter who opens this repository
should conclude three things about them (their words: "que soy capaz de entender un ML, relacionarlo
con el mercado financiero y automatizarlo"):

1. **They understand machine learning**: the model is compared with simple baselines, validated
   without look-ahead, explained (which variables drive it), and its results are reported honestly
   even when it does not win.
2. **They connect it to markets**: results are read the way a portfolio manager reads them
   (return, risk, drawdown, costs, turnover, sector concentration, Fama-French attribution: alpha or
   just factor exposure?).
3. **They automate it**: a *live* portfolio that rebuilds itself every month, publishes its
   holdings and an out-of-sample track record, and alerts the owner when an update fails.

## 2. Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- Monthly ranking of S&P 500 stocks by a gradient-boosting model trained on factor characteristics.
- A long-only portfolio a private-banking client could hold: about 50 stocks, equal weight, monthly
  rebalance, transaction costs deducted.
- A walk-forward backtest (historical) and a live track record (from the first automated month on),
  always compared with the S&P 500 and with a simple factor rule.
- Factor attribution (Fama-French 5 factors + momentum) and model explanation (SHAP) in business words.
- Full automation on GitHub Actions with quality gates and a failure alert.
- The common DAIS layer: star schema (DuckDB), Streamlit dashboard, data dictionary, pandera checks,
  lineage, business requirements and backlog, tests and CI badge, README business-first with a
  "Résumé en français".

**Non-goals**
- Long-short or leveraged portfolios, intraday data, options.
- An LLM component (this project's AI is the ranking model; no API key anywhere).
- Investment advice. The app carries a disclaimer.
- Paid data.

**Anti-goals (failure even if it "works")**
- Any look-ahead: a feature, a membership or a fundamental used before it was public.
- A live track record that can be rewritten after the fact.
- Publishing a portfolio built from broken or incomplete data instead of stopping and alerting.
- Overstating results: hiding that the ML lost to a baseline, or hiding survivorship bias.

## 3. Constraints

- Free data only; the job must run on GitHub-hosted runners without the owner's machine.
- The owner's git token has no `workflow` scope: workflow files are created in the GitHub web editor
  (as for projects 1-4); every other push is normal git.
- The owner asked for no background tasks during development sessions (CI is polled in the foreground).
- The SEC requires a contact in the User-Agent. Locally it is read from `.env` (as in project 3); in
  CI from a repository secret `SEC_CONTACT`. The contact never appears in the repository.
- Yahoo Finance (yfinance) rate-limits by IP and publishes no limits; shared runner IPs can be blocked
  for hours. Stooq now needs a captcha key; Tiingo's free plan is internal-use only.
- Sober visual theme shared by the portfolio apps (several companies, no single brand): navy, black,
  white, the validated 8-colour chart palette.

## 6. Design

### 6.1 Calendar and universe
- Monthly grain; month-end prices; the portfolio for month M+1 is formed from data available at the
  close of month M.
- Universe at month M = S&P 500 members at month-end M, rebuilt backwards from today's list and the
  changes table. Tickers are mapped to SEC CIKs (SEC `company_tickers.json`, plus a small manual map
  for renamed or re-used tickers, kept in `config/`).
- History: prices from 2005; features need fundamentals, which XBRL covers from mid-2009 for large
  filers, so the first feature month is 2010-06 and the first out-of-sample month is 2013-01 (three
  years of training). The backtest runs 2013-01 to the last closed month.
- Survivorship: the share of past members (count and index weight proxy) without Yahoo prices is
  computed every run and shown in the data-quality page and the README.

### 6.2 Features (point-in-time, about 15)
- Price-based: momentum 12-1 and 6-1 months, 1-month reversal, 12-month volatility, 1-year beta,
  size (log market cap = price x shares outstanding from XBRL `dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding`).
- Fundamentals, each used only from its SEC `filed` date: book-to-market, earnings yield,
  sales-to-price, ROE, gross profitability, accruals, leverage, asset growth.
- Sector from the SEC SIC code mapped to about 11 sectors (config file).
- Each feature is ranked cross-sectionally within the month (0-1), so the model compares stocks
  with each other, not with history; missing values stay missing (the model handles them).

### 6.3 Target, model, baselines
- Target: next-month total return minus the cross-sectional median (what matters is ranking).
- Model: LightGBM regressor, walk-forward: retrained every month on all months before it, with
  fixed hyper-parameters chosen once on 2010-2012 and frozen (stated in the methodology).
  LightGBM on the owner's Mac needs `libomp` (Homebrew); CI on Ubuntu needs nothing extra.
- Baselines, scored on the same months and the same portfolio rules:
  1. S&P 500 total return (`^SP500TR`);
  2. equal-weight universe (all members);
  3. simple factor rule: average rank of 12-1 momentum, gross profitability and book-to-market, top 50.
- Model quality: monthly information coefficient (Spearman rank correlation of prediction vs
  realised return), its mean and t-statistic, and the return spread between top and bottom deciles.

### 6.4 Portfolio and performance
- Top 50 by predicted rank, equal weight, monthly rebalance; costs 10 bps per side on turnover
  (config).
- Metrics: annualised return and volatility, Sharpe (Fama-French risk-free rate), maximum drawdown,
  turnover, tracking error and information ratio vs the S&P 500, hit rate vs the index, sector weights
  over time.
- Attribution: regression of monthly excess returns on Fama-French 5 factors + momentum, with
  Newey-West standard errors; alpha and loadings shown with plain-English reading ("most of the
  excess return is explained by the momentum tilt"). French data lags by about a month, so attribution
  uses the months available.
- Explanation: SHAP values of each monthly model; global importance over time and, for the current
  portfolio, the top reasons each holding was selected.

### 6.5 Live track record
- Starts with the first automated run. Each month appends one row (holdings, realised return of the
  previous month's portfolio, benchmark returns). The run refuses to change existing rows; a test
  checks that the committed history is a prefix of the new one.
- Backtest and live record are always shown separately.

### 6.6 Automation (GitHub Actions)
- Schedule: cron on days 1-3 of each month (UTC); the job exits early when the month is already
  done, so it effectively runs on the first business day with complete data. Also
  `workflow_dispatch` for a manual re-run.
- Steps: refresh membership, prices (last month, batched, retries with exponential back-off),
  fundamentals (new SEC filings only) and French factors -> quality gates -> features -> retrain ->
  rank -> portfolio -> live record -> attribution and SHAP -> report -> commit to `main` as the
  Actions bot -> Streamlit Cloud redeploys from the commit.
- Quality gates (pandera + checks); any failure stops the run before anything is written:
  prices for at least 95% of current members; no monthly return beyond +/-80% without a split or
  dividend explanation; membership count 495-510; month-end date is a closed month; feature
  coverage above thresholds; fundamentals not newer than their filing date.
- Alert: on failure, a final step opens a GitHub issue labelled `refresh-failed` with the failed
  step and the run link (or comments on the open one, so failures do not pile up); GitHub sends it
  to the owner by e-mail and mobile. The issue is closed automatically by the next successful run.
  GitHub's own e-mail for a failed scheduled run also reaches the owner.
- Permissions: `contents: write`, `issues: write` with the built-in `GITHUB_TOKEN`; one secret,
  `SEC_CONTACT`.

### 6.7 Data model (DuckDB over parquet in `data/snapshot/`)
- Dimensions: `dim_month`, `dim_security` (ticker, CIK, name, sector), `dim_sector`.
- Facts: `fact_membership` (month x security), `fact_prices` (month x security: close, total
  return), `fact_fundamentals` (security x period, with `filed` date), `fact_features`,
  `fact_predictions` (month x security: score, rank, model version), `fact_holdings` (month x
  security: weight, source = backtest/live), `fact_performance` (month x strategy: return, costs),
  `fact_factors` (month: FF5 + momentum + rf), `fact_shap`.
- Raw downloads (Yahoo, SEC JSON) are not committed; the snapshot keeps derived monthly tables only.

### 6.8 Dashboard (Streamlit, sober theme)
Pages: Current portfolio (holdings, sector mix, why each stock was picked); Live track record;
Backtest (model vs S&P 500 vs factor rule vs equal weight, drawdowns, costs); What drives the model
(SHAP over time); Factor attribution; Data quality and refresh log (last run, gates, survivorship
share); About (method, limits, disclaimer).

### 6.9 Documentation and tests
- `docs/business_requirements.md` (personas: portfolio manager, private banker, risk/compliance;
  user stories with acceptance criteria; KPI definitions), backlog in it; `docs/data_dictionary.md`
  with lineage; `docs/data_model.md`; `docs/methodology.md` (ML and finance in business language).
- Tests: no-look-ahead (a feature at month M never uses data after M; fundamentals respect `filed`),
  membership reconstruction on known changes, portfolio weights and costs, performance metrics on
  hand-computed cases, attribution on synthetic factor data, live-record append-only, quality gates
  fail on bad fixtures, the alert step's issue body. CI runs on the committed snapshot without network.
- README: business problem, live results, solution, tech, limits, Résumé en français.

### 6.10 Quality bar and the skills used at each stage (owner's request)
The owner asked for every skill needed so the research, code, writing, app and slides are as
professional and clean as possible:
- Research and finance method: `financial-intelligence` (factor definitions, attribution, metrics),
  `academic-research` (sources for each factor and for the walk-forward and SHAP method, cited in
  the methodology), `data:statistical-analysis` (IC t-statistics, Newey-West, significance wording).
- Code: `superpowers:writing-plans` then `superpowers:test-driven-development` for the core
  (no-look-ahead, membership, portfolio, attribution), `superpowers:verification-before-completion`
  before claiming a step done, and a code review before publishing.
- Writing: `humanizer` on the README, docs and app text, so they read as written by the owner.
- App and charts: `dataviz` (form, palette validation, marks) with the shared sober theme.
- Slides: a short deck of the strategy and its results (`anthropic-skills:pptx`), same palette.

## 8. Calls made in this document (owner to check)
- First out-of-sample month 2013-01 (XBRL coverage drives it).
- 10 bps per side transaction cost; no sector cap (sector weights reported instead).
- Monthly retraining with frozen hyper-parameters (simpler to explain, avoids tuning on the test).
- Alert as a GitHub issue plus GitHub's own failure e-mail; auto-close on the next success.
- Derived tables committed, raw downloads not (Yahoo and SEC terms).
